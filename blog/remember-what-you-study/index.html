Why do I forget what I studied last week?

Forgetting is normal. In the 1880s, Hermann Ebbinghaus measured how quickly he forgot lists he had memorized, and found most of it went in the first day or two. That shape, now called the forgetting curve, shows up again and again.

The other problem is how we study. Rereading notes and highlighting feel productive because the material looks familiar. But recognizing something is not the same as being able to recall it on a test.

What is active recall?

Active recall means pulling information out of your head instead of putting it back in. Close the book and answer a question. Explain a concept out loud. Do a practice problem before looking at the worked example.

It's one of the best-supported study methods. In Roediger and Karpicke's 2006 study, students who practiced recalling a passage remembered much more a week later than students who spent the same time rereading it, even though the rereaders felt more confident.

What is spaced repetition?

Spaced repetition means reviewing something just as you're about to forget it, with the gaps getting longer each time: a day, a few days, a week, a few weeks. A large review of hundreds of experiments found that spreading study out beats cramming the same amount of time into one session.

Put the two together, testing yourself on a schedule, and you have the core of almost every serious memory method.

Why ask the same idea a different way?

If you review with the exact same flashcard every time, you can end up memorizing the card instead of the idea. You know that card 14's answer is "mitochondria" without really understanding why.

Asking the same concept in a new form (a different example, a word problem instead of a definition, "which of these is not…") checks that you understand the idea itself. If you can answer it three different ways, you probably know it.

How do I do this without making flashcards?

Anki is powerful, but building and maintaining a deck takes time, and many people quit before the reviews pay off. Some lighter options:

  • Blank-page review: after class, write everything you remember on a blank page, then check your notes and fill the gaps.
  • Question-first notes: turn each heading in your notes into a question and cover the answer when you review.

Whatever you use, the rule is the same: test yourself, space it out, and change the question. Pair it with fewer distractions (see how to stop checking your phone while studying) and you'll remember far more from the same hours.

FAQ

Is Anki better than other methods?

Anki is a great spaced repetition tool if you keep it up. The method matters more than the app: test yourself and space out reviews. Use whatever you will actually stick with.

How long should I wait between reviews?

A common pattern is about 1 day, then 3 days, then a week, then 3 weeks, shortening the gap whenever you get something wrong.

Does rereading notes work at all?

It helps a little, mostly the first time. After that, closing your notes and recalling the material does much more for the same time.
