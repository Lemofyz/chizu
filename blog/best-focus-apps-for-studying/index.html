How do you pick a focus app that actually works for you?

Most "best focus apps" lists throw twenty apps at you. The better question is what is actually stopping you. If you can't start, a blocker won't help. If your phone is the problem, a planner won't help. Pick the section below that sounds like you, try one app for a week, and drop it if it doesn't stick.

Heads up: we make Chizu, one of the apps below. We've tried to describe every app fairly, including the ones that compete with ours. Prices change often, so check each app's page.

Which apps help if you study better with company?

  • Focusmate: book a video session with another person, say your goals at the start, then both work quietly. One of the most-recommended body doubling tools.
  • Flow Club: hosted group focus sessions with video, a timer and music. More of a room than a one-on-one.
  • Discord study servers and "study with me" videos: free, low-pressure company, if you can resist the chat.
  • Chizu: if a stranger on camera feels like too much, a small digital cat sits with you instead. She can't see you, but she notices when you leave to scroll.

Which apps help if your phone keeps winning?

  • Forest: plant a virtual tree that grows while you stay off your phone. Leave to use other apps and it dies. Simple and still one of the most popular.
  • one sec: makes you pause for a breath before opening the apps you choose. Small friction, surprisingly effective.
  • Opal: blocks distracting apps on a schedule or during focus sessions.
  • Freedom: blocks sites and apps across your phone and computer at the same time, useful if you just switch devices.
  • Free option: your phone's own Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing limits, plus grayscale mode. More ideas in how to focus on studying when your phone keeps pulling you away.

What are some apps like Finch for studying?

Finch is a self-care app where a little bird grows as you take care of yourself. People love it because caring for a cute creature makes habits feel less like chores. If you want that feeling for studying specifically:

  • Study Bunny: a study timer with a bunny companion who cheers you on.
  • Focus-timer pets like Focus Dog or StudyPet: a pet that grows or gets rewards while you focus.
  • Chizu: a digital desk pet based on a real cat. She sits with you during focus sessions, reacts when you pat her, sulks when you pick up your phone, and quizzes you on what you're studying.

Which apps help you remember what you studied?

  • Anki: the classic spaced repetition flashcard app. Very powerful, takes effort to set up and keep up.
  • Quizlet: flashcards and study sets, easy to start, many shared decks.
  • RemNote: notes that turn into flashcards with spaced repetition built in.
  • Chizu: tell her what you're studying and she asks you questions, then brings back what you missed days later in a new form. No decks to build.

Why these methods work: active recall and spaced repetition.

FAQ

What is the best free focus app?

It depends on the problem. For blocking your phone, your phone's built-in Screen Time limits are free. For company, Discord study servers and study-with-me videos are free. Many apps on this list have free versions or free demos.

What is a good alternative to Forest?

If you like the game feel, try a focus pet app such as Focus Dog or Study Bunny. If you need harder limits, one sec, Opal or Freedom. If you need company, Focusmate or Chizu.

Do focus apps work for ADHD?

Many people with ADHD find body doubling apps and visual timers especially helpful. See our ADHD study tips for more.
